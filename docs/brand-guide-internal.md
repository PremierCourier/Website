Markdown export of the internal brand guide for use by Claude Code and the build. The engagement's operating constraints live in `CLAUDE.md` (Non-negotiables); they are intentionally not repeated here. Approved facts, restricted claims, and verbatim copy live in `content-rules.md`.

## 00 How to Use This Guide

*This document is the authoritative brand and marketing context for humans and AI systems producing Premier Courier content.*

> **SOURCE OF TRUTH RULE When creating Premier Courier marketing material, use this guide before relying on old website copy, prior drafts, generic logistics language, or assumptions.**

### Purpose

This guide exists so a writer, designer, social media manager, or AI system can produce on-brand Premier Courier content without the company being re-explained each time. It captures what Premier Courier does, who it serves, how it sounds, how it looks, what may be claimed, and the operating constraints of this engagement.

### Precedence Rules for AI and Content Creators

1.  Follow the explicit request or content brief, provided it does not conflict with the claims, accuracy, privacy, or operating rules in this guide.

2.  Protect the brand boundary: Premier Courier is a local, owner-run, medical-first courier. Do not drift into national-logistics or freight-company positioning.

3.  Use the service hierarchy, audience matrix, and pain signals to tailor the message.

4.  Use the approved voice, words, palette, typography, and imagery rules.

5.  Never invent client names, facility names, volumes, on-time percentages, certifications, awards, partnerships, or driver quotes.

6.  Never publish anything that identifies a patient, a specimen, a facility’s internal process, or a healthcare client without written approval. HIPAA exposure is the single largest content risk for this brand.

7.  Follow the non-negotiables in CLAUDE.md without exception.

## 01 Brand Foundation

*The core identity every Premier Courier message must preserve.*

### Brand Mission

Premier Courier Services gets time-sensitive medical and business deliveries where they need to be — on time, every time, with the personal care of a small, owner-run Arizona business.

### Brand Promise

Every item is treated with the same care regardless of its value. The phone is answered by the owner, day or night. Drivers are meticulous, HIPAA-compliant, and attentive. Service runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including holidays.

### Primary Positioning

Premier Courier Services is the trusted medical and business courier for Central and Northern Arizona — the courier hospitals, labs, and pharmacies rely on when the delivery cannot wait and cannot go wrong.

> **APPROVED PRIMARY BRAND LINE Arizona’s Trusted Courier. On Time, Every Time.**

### Origin Story (approved for public use)

Premier Courier was founded by Travis Womack, an entrepreneur from childhood, whose relationship with a local hospital in the early 2000s became the foundation of the business. He grew it into a trusted name across Central and Northern Arizona over two decades and passed away in 2014. Alanna Hanigan has owned and operated the company since 2014 and carries that legacy forward.

*Use the story with restraint. It belongs on the About page, in anniversary or milestone posts, and in “who we are” content. It does not belong in service promotions or quote CTAs.*

### The Owner Is the Brand

Alanna is personally involved in daily operations, knows customers individually, and keeps her phone with her for any delivery need. She does not use an answering service by choice. This is the brand’s primary differentiator against larger couriers and must remain visible in the voice, the About content, and the customer-service claims.

## 07 Voice & Writing Style

*How Premier Courier sounds in every channel.*

### Write Like

- A capable owner-operator who has done this run a thousand times.

- A neighbor who happens to run a very professional business.

- Someone who is proud of the drivers and the work, and says so plainly.

### Two Registers

| **Register**              | **When**                                                                                      | **Rule**                                                                              |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **First person (Alanna)** | About page, LinkedIn personal posts, Facebook community posts, replies to reviews.            | “I” and “my drivers.” Warm, direct, specific. Never ghost-written to sound corporate. |
| **Company voice**         | Website service pages, Google Business Profile, Yelp description, LinkedIn company page, ads. | “We.” Still warm, but tighter and more declarative.                                   |

### Core Writing Characteristics

| **Characteristic** | **Rule**                                                                                                                    |
|--------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Clear**          | Plain words. Say what is picked up, where it goes, and when.                                                                |
| **Specific**       | Name the delivery type (specimens, trays, filings), the hour, the county — not ‘packages’ and ‘Arizona’.                    |
| **Warm**           | Sound like a person. Contractions are fine. Exclamation points: at most one per piece, and never in healthcare-facing copy. |
| **Calm**           | Urgency is the customer’s problem; composure is the brand’s answer. Never breathless.                                       |
| **Honest**         | No superlatives you cannot back. Say ‘trusted,’ not ‘#1’.                                                                   |

### Preferred Words

On time; every time; trusted; meticulous; careful; discreet; HIPAA-compliant; 24/7; including holidays; owner; drivers; route; pickup; delivery; specimen; tray; same-day; scheduled; urgent; Prescott; Central and Northern Arizona; local; personal; reliable.

### Avoid or Limit

Logistics solutions; last-mile; leverage; seamless; cutting-edge; state-of-the-art; real-time tracking (until the capability exists and is approved); #1; best in Arizona; guaranteed (unless a written guarantee exists); packages (prefer the specific item); synergy; game changer; disrupt.

### Example: Generic vs. Premier Courier

| **Generic Courier Copy**                                                | **Premier Courier Copy**                                                                              |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **We provide fast, reliable delivery solutions across Arizona.**        | Specimens picked up on the lab’s schedule and delivered on time — 24 hours a day, including holidays. |
| **Our state-of-the-art logistics network ensures on-time performance.** | Same drivers, same routes, same owner on the phone. That is how we stay on time.                      |
| **Contact our team today!**                                             | Call (928) 533-3585. Alanna answers.                                                                  |

> **SENTENCE TEST Could this sentence belong to any courier company? If yes, add the item, the hour, the place, or the person.**

## 08 Visual Identity System

*The visual tokens derived from the existing logo. The logo is fixed; everything else is built to match it.*

### Logo Usage

- Use the official logo only: the blue-gradient ‘P’ mark with the serif PREMIER wordmark and the ‘COURIER SERVICES, LLC’ line.

- Preferred placement: top left on web and documents; bottom right on social graphics; centered when used alone.

- Maintain clear space equal to the height of the ‘P’ mark on all sides.

- Do not stretch, distort, recolor, rotate, outline, add effects to, or rebuild the logo with substitute fonts.

- On dark or photographic backgrounds, place the logo on a white rounded panel. Do not place it directly on busy imagery.

- Do not pair the logo with any other company’s or vendor’s branding in any client-facing asset.

> **ASSET LIMITATION The only logo file available is a 320×132 px PNG with a transparent background and a soft dark edge shadow (so it still belongs on white or on a white panel, never directly on navy). It is adequate for web, social, and email signatures at native size or smaller. It is not adequate for print, vehicle graphics, or large-format use. Do not upscale it. If a print asset is required, the logo must be re-traced as vector first and approved by Alanna.**

### Palette Rules

- Blue carries the brand. Courier Blue is the primary; Deep Blue and Navy Ink provide depth; Sky Blue provides light.

- No UI gradients (v1.1). Hero bands and buttons use flat Courier Blue, Deep Blue, or Navy Ink. The gradient lives only in the logo itself.

- Copper is an optional accent added in this guide, not a logo color. It exists to make ‘Call Now’ and 24/7 cues stand out from a blue page. One copper element per asset, maximum. Omit it entirely if in doubt.

- Text is Navy Ink or Slate on white or Cloud. Never Sky Blue or Steel Blue text on white.

- Keep at least 60% white or Cloud space in every layout.

### Shape & Layout

- Corner radius 8 px for cards and buttons. One radius across the whole system.

- Soft neutral shadows only: 0 2px 12px rgba(11,53,86,0.08).

- One dominant message per graphic, section, or post.

- Prefer cards, simple stat rows (verified only), two-column comparisons, and short lists over dense text.

## 09 Imagery & Content Structure

*What to show, how to render it, and what is off-limits.*

### Premier Courier’s Visual World: The Route

The brand’s world is the Arizona road between a facility and a lab — high-desert light, the Prescott pines, Highway 69 and I-17, a driver’s hands on a sealed transport container, a signed delivery log, dawn and late-night runs. Every image should feel like it was taken on a real route, not selected from a library.

### Visuals to Use

- Real photographs of Alanna, the drivers, and the vehicles (with their consent) — these are the brand’s strongest assets and the reason to prefer photography over illustration.

- Arizona landscapes and townscapes: Prescott courthouse plaza, Granite Dells, Sedona red rock, Flagstaff pines, desert highways at golden hour.

- Sealed transport coolers and containers, closed document envelopes, signature pads — always closed, always unlabeled.

- Simple route or coverage maps of the five counties.

- Clean typographic graphics: one statement, the logo, generous white space.

### Visuals to Avoid

- Any specimen, tube, bag, or container with a visible label, barcode, or patient information.

- Any identifiable hospital, lab, pharmacy, or clinic interior or signage without written permission.

- Stock imagery of smiling delivery workers, handshakes, generic vans, or cardboard boxes.

- Futuristic tracking dashboards, GPS map overlays, or ‘tech’ imagery — the capability is not established and the aesthetic is off-brand.

- Cartoon illustrations, emoji-heavy designs, and generic or abstract 3D renders. (v1.1: 3D renders of Premier Courier's own objects are allowed — the sealed, unlabeled blue transport cooler with the P mark, and a relief map of the five counties.)

- Unsplash-style generic photography as a permanent solution. Acceptable as a placeholder until real photography exists.

### Photographs of Alanna and the Drivers

> **RULE All images of people are real photographs taken with their consent. AI tools may be used only to edit a real photograph: retouching, background cleanup, extending a crop, resizing to a platform format, and matching color across a set. AI may not generate a person, add or remove a person, item, or vehicle, or place anyone in a location or situation that was not photographed. Platform AI labels are never removed. Reference photos are handled only in tools the team controls.**

The reason this rule exists: Alanna’s customers know her face and her drivers by name. A generated likeness of any of them, even with consent, trades away the one thing larger couriers cannot copy — that the people in the pictures are the people at the dock.

### Render Style

Photography: natural light, honest color, minimal editing. Graphics: flat, clean, white-dominant, one blue accent band or block, the logo, and at most one short line of text. If a graphic would look at home on a national parcel carrier’s feed, it is wrong.

### Icons

Structural icons that identify an object (a phone for the phone number, a clock for 24/7, a map pin for the service area) are fine. Decorative concept icons (a shield for ‘trust’, a heart for ‘care’, a lightning bolt for ‘fast’) are limited to one per asset. Zero is better.

## 13 AI Content Generation Rules

*Operating instructions when this guide is provided to an AI system.*

> **CONTEXT You are creating marketing for Premier Courier Services, LLC, an owner-run medical and business courier based in Prescott, Arizona, serving Central and Northern Arizona 24/7 including holidays. Its differentiators are reliability, careful HIPAA-compliant handling, and the owner answering the phone personally.**

### AI Must Understand

- Medical transport leads; business deliveries follow.

- The owner is the brand. First-person content is Alanna’s voice and must sound like a person, not a company.

- Trust is built through specifics and consistency, not superlatives.

- Privacy is a content constraint, not a compliance footnote.

### AI Must Never

- Name clients, facilities, or patients, or describe an identifiable delivery.

- Claim tracking technology, certifications, guarantees, rankings, or metrics not listed as approved.

- Mention the content team, any vendor, any software, or any dispute.

- Use national-carrier or gig-app language, or tech-forward imagery.

- Produce more than one exclamation point per asset, or any in healthcare-facing copy.

- Publish anything before Alanna approves it.

### AI Workflow Before Producing Content

13. Identify the channel and register (first person or company voice).

14. Identify the audience from the matrix in Section 4.

15. Choose one pain signal or theme. One idea per asset.

16. Use only approved facts from Section 11.

17. Apply the palette, typography, and imagery rules.

18. Run the privacy checklist.

19. Run the Brand Test in Section 14 before delivering the draft for approval.

> **DEFAULT CONTENT FORMULA A real moment or pain the audience recognizes → what Premier Courier does about it, specifically → the human behind it → one direct CTA.**

20. Would Alanna say this in her own words?

21. Does it name the item, the hour, the place, or the person — or is it generic?

22. Is medical transport leading, unless the piece is specifically about business deliveries?

24. Could anything in it identify a client, facility, or patient?

25. Does it use the logo blues, the approved fonts, and real or place-based imagery?

26. Is there one idea and one CTA?

27. Does it contain anything that reveals the content team or a vendor?
